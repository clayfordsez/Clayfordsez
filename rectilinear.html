01 Overview

Both cabinets went in for a tweeter-pot repair and full crossover recap. What started as a stuck rheostat turned into a full driver-level diagnosis on both channels, a from-scratch sub-board relocation to work around glued-in crossover boards, and — ultimately — a full 8-tweeter replacement project once testing showed the original Peerless drivers had failed on both sides.

Crossovers are glued into both cabinets with an old silicone/RTV bead. Both baffles were removed for full access, which turned out to make everything downstream far easier.
Drivers connect to the crossover boards via spade-clip terminal strips on the baffle, not hard-wired — this made isolating branches for testing (and swapping components between channels for diagnosis) straightforward.
No official factory schematic for this speaker has ever surfaced publicly — confirmed via multiple vintage-audio forum threads (Audio Asylum, Classic Speaker Pages) going back over 20 years. The network topology used throughout this rebuild was reverse-engineered by hand from the physical board, and later cross-checked against a schematic sourced from a Classic Speaker Pages forum post — see §05.

02 Right Channel — Findings & Repairs

Tweeter pot resolved

Original rheostat mechanically broken — wiper snags and drops rather than sweeping cleanly. Confirmed via meter across all three lugs before replacement.
Found an undocumented 8Ω 5W resistor bridging the wiper lug and one fixed-end lug on the original pot — not on the recap parts list, part of the original L-pad design. Metered at 8.6Ω (within ±10% tolerance) and reused as-is.
Replaced with a new Jameco wirewound rheostat, wired to match the original's lug configuration (verified empirically per-part, not assumed from physical position).

Midrange pot needs part

Also confirmed bad on testing. Replacement rheostat not yet sourced — no spare beyond the two ordered for the tweeter position.

Woofer Zobel network part needed

Separate 8Ω 10W resistor found on a terminal strip alongside the 60µF woofer cap — a Zobel network across the woofer terminals. Original metered at 6.6Ω isolated (out of the ±10% window), needs replacement.
Replacement selected: uxcell 25W 8Ω aluminum-housed wirewound resistor, screw-tab terminals. Oversized on wattage deliberately (25W vs. original 10W) — safe headroom, no downside besides physical size.

60µF woofer cap resolved

Original tested at 87µF against a 60µF nominal — roughly +45% drift, consistent with age-related degradation (possible internal leakage). Replaced with new Dayton DMPC 60µF from the recap order.

The 33Ω resistor saga resolved

Madisound's "33Ω 25W" order arrived stamped 25W 3Ω3 J — actually 3.3Ω. Confirmed this wasn't a fulfillment error but a listing error on Madisound's own site: their product photo for that exact SKU shows a resistor stamped 3.3Ω, not 33Ω.
After fully tracing the rebuilt harness, determined the 33Ω resistor was never part of the actual circuit at all — a leftover artifact from an earlier, discarded plan to delete the tweeter pot and replace it with a fixed resistor. That plan was reversed in favor of repairing the pot, but the resistor stayed on the parts list. Pursuing a refund from Madisound rather than a replacement.

Sub-board relocation resolved

New caps (particularly the 100µF Solen film caps) are physically larger than the original electrolytic can they replace — the original board footprint couldn't accommodate them.
Freed the original terminal strip (holding the woofer cap + Zobel resistor) by drilling out its mounting rivet, then reused the strip itself as the new sub-board — no fabrication needed. Remounted to an internal brace near the cabinet back; new large cap zip-tied to the same brace with padding to prevent rattle.

Woofer mounting left as-is

Right channel woofer sits recessed with no routed rabbet in the baffle — likely mounted from the rear through the original hole, rather than a deliberate flush conversion. This differs from the left channel (front-mounted, routed rabbet — see below). Decided to leave the mismatch rather than buy a router to correct it.

03 Left Channel — Findings

Harness fully wired but not yet finalized pending tweeter sourcing. Status: paused.

Woofer inductor resolved

Found knocked loose from its mounting post. Root cause traced: the woofer was removed for service at some point in the speaker's history and fell back into the cabinet, physically striking the inductor. Confirmed electrically undamaged — reads the same resistance as the matching right-channel inductor. Needs remounting only, same orientation as original (to avoid introducing new magnetic coupling with the neighboring inductor).

Woofer mounting reference point

Front-mounted with a routed baffle rabbet — matches the documented original factory configuration for this speaker (front-mounted woofers with cloth surrounds). This is the channel that appears period-correct; the right channel is the likely deviation.

04 Tweeter Replacement — Options Considered

Originals identified as Peerless MT-20 (2" super tweeter) and Peerless MT-25 (2.5" main tweeter) — Danish manufacturer, matching the "Made in Denmark" stamp on the original housings, confirmed via a forum thread naming this exact family as used in Rectilinear speakers by name.

Calculated design crossover point for the tweeter branch: ~10kHz, from the 2.0µF coupling cap into the ~8Ω branch impedance (f ≈ 1/(2πRC)). Used throughout as the benchmark against each candidate's Fs and recommended minimum crossover.
Measured original baffle cutouts directly: super tweeter position is 1⅞" (47.6mm) — smaller than the "2 inch" nominal class name the MT-20 is sold under. Main tweeter position measures 2.5" (63.5mm) exactly. All cutout comparisons below are against these real measurements, not the rounded nominal sizes.

Why each was passed over

Dayton DC28F-8 — excellent spec match and the best Fs headroom of any option, but the largest required cutout and repeatedly back-ordered (Parts Express estimate: 8/14).
Ciare HT050-8 — a strong physical fit (50mm against a real 1⅞" hole) and genuinely cheap, but DIY-audio community sentiment on Ciare's tweeters specifically is mixed-to-negative, in contrast to more consistent praise for their woofers/mids.
Peerless CT-62 — the most period-correct option and a genuinely live, active listing (99 sold, reputable seller) — but only available in 4Ω. Adding series resistance to compensate was considered but set aside as an imprecise fix without a verified target impedance to compensate against.
Goldwood GT-25 — ruled out outright: wrong size class (3.5" driver, 79mm cutout) and voiced for PA/DJ use, not hi-fi.

Leading candidates: Visaton TW70-8 + TW6NG8 leading — not yet decided

A two-part combo, matching the original design's two-size architecture rather than one uniform driver across all 8 positions. Same manufacturer and product family for both, giving consistent voicing between positions. The TW70's cutout (64mm) is a near-exact match to the measured 63.5mm main-tweeter hole — the best physical fit found for either position across the whole search, likely needing little to no enlargement. The TW6NG still needs some enlargement on the smaller position. Both currently in stock at Parts Express, versus Vifa's back-order.

Genuinely open questions before committing: cone material isn't explicitly stated on either datasheet (same ambiguity that affected other candidates). The two parts use different terminal sizes (4.8mm vs. 2.8mm spades), so different connectors are needed for each. The frequency-response graphs on both datasheets haven't been closely cross-checked against each other or against the originals' likely curve — the numbers above come from the tabulated specs, not a curve-by-curve comparison.

Remaining alternative: Vifa / Peerless D19TD05-08 alternative

Still a real option — closest sensitivity match to the originals (87.5dB vs. ~88.5dB) and the strongest brand pedigree (Vifa and Peerless are sister Danish companies, now under the same parent group), with real 5-star reviews from owners describing actual vintage-speaker restorations. Downside: larger cutout (70mm) than the Visaton combo needs on either position, and currently back-ordered until 8/21 versus the Visaton parts' in-stock availability.

No final selection has been made. This section reflects where the research stands, not a purchase decision.

05 Reference Schematic

Sourced from a Classic Speaker Pages forum post ("Rectilinear iii 1969"). Drawn in DesignWorks Lite, dated December 2006. This appears to be the only documentation of this network's actual topology to surface publicly — the same speaker has an active, unanswered "does anyone have the schematic" thread on Audio Asylum dating back to 2004.

Rectilinear III (1969) — crossover network Download original PDF · Source: Classic Speaker Pages forum upload

What it confirms against the hand-traced harness

Woofer branch: series inductor into the driver, with a resistor + capacitor Zobel network wired in parallel directly across the woofer terminals — matches the 8Ω/60µF pairing found and traced on the physical board.
Midrange branch: its own L-pad pot feeding the whizzer-cone midrange through a band-pass network.
Tweeter branch: L-pad pot with a parallel resistor bridging wiper-to-one-end (matching the exact topology traced by hand tonight), feeding through a coupling cap into all four tweeters wired in series — confirming the series wiring determined empirically through direct testing.
