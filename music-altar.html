01 Overview

The goal was to turn a plain industrial rack into one dedicated piece of furniture for the turntable, receiver, records, and guitar amp. The rack already had the right structure and load capacity; the work was mostly about proportion, finish, storage, and light.

The finished piece uses a metallic-gold frame, black handmade record dividers, and concealed warm-white LEDs under the front edge of every shelf. The lower bay was deliberately left 23 inches tall so the Vox AC15C1 can tilt forward for access to its rear-top controls.

Design rule: the lighting should illuminate the gear and catch the gold finish without showing obvious LED dots or turning the rack into a display case.

02 Frame & Finish

The rack began as powder-coated steel. The frame was scuffed with Scotch-Brite, sprayed with white primer, and finished in metallic gold. High humidity and a few heavy primer spots left small soft globs; those were allowed to cure, rubbed or sanded away, and feathered before the gold coats went on.

The gold was the decisive choice. In ordinary daylight it reads closer to aged brass than bright decorative gold. Under the 2700K LEDs it warms up and visually connects the vintage receiver, black hardware, and wood speaker cabinets.

03 Handmade Record Dividers

Six V-shaped dividers were made from 5/16-inch steel round rod. Each divider is approximately 9 inches tall with a 7-inch base. The rods were cut to length, bent by hand over the patio stair handrail, checked against one another for uniformity, then covered in thin-wall black heat-shrink tubing.

Six divider centerlines were laid out at approximately 1.0, 7.7, 14.4, 21.1, 27.8, and 34.5 in. across the 35.5-inch shelf.
The repeated Vs divide the shelf into five roughly 6.7-inch bays, making a partially filled collection look intentional while keeping the records upright.
The black heat-shrink creates a smooth jacket-safe surface and makes the hand-formed steel visually disappear behind the records.

The retainer problem Resolved

The original plan was to retain the smooth rod ends with axle hats. Cutting had pinched and deformed the ends enough that the hats — and later solid shaft collars — would not pass over them. Two-piece collars solved the geometry but were too expensive for twelve mounting points.

The permanent answer was simpler: each leg passes through the particleboard shelf, then through a large fender washer bonded underneath with J-B Weld. The washer spreads lateral load over a wide area; the epoxy locks the rod, washer, and shelf together. The ugly cut ends remain hidden underneath and no longer matter.

04 Warm-White Shelf Lighting

A 16.4-foot, 24V COB LED kit supplies four cut sections mounted beneath the front edges of the shelves. The strip is 8 mm wide, single-color, and rated at 2700K. COB construction keeps the light visually continuous without an aluminum diffuser channel.

Each run was cut just under the shelf width, tested before mounting, and placed roughly 1/2 to 3/4 inch behind the front edge. The result is a downward wash across the gear and record jackets rather than a visible row of LEDs.

The final brightness is intentionally restrained. With the room lamp off, the LEDs become the mood lighting; with the lamp on, they read as subtle shelf illumination.

05 Final Setup

The current stack places the Sansui turntable on top, the Yamaha CR-800 receiver below, records in the divided shelf, and the Vox AC15C1 in the tall lower bay. The amp opening is visually generous, but the extra space is functional: the Vox controls sit at the rear of the top panel, so the cabinet must be able to tilt forward or slide out easily.

The first record after the build session was Isaac Hayes’ Black Moses. That was the point the rack stopped feeling like a utility shelf and became the music altar.

“Listening to Black Moses and staring at my altar.”
